EYE TRACKING ANALYTICAL METHODS GUIDE
======================================
Experiment: Personality Perception from Face Videos (Pairwise Comparison)
Hardware: EyeLink 1000 Plus @ 1000 Hz
Design: Sequential video presentation, 51 participants, 125 trials each

========================================================================
METHOD 1: CORE FIXATION METRICS (START HERE)
========================================================================

PURPOSE:
  Basic measures of where and how long people look. These are the foundation
  for all other analyses.

METRICS:
  - Total fixation count: number of fixations per video per trial
  - Mean fixation duration (ms): average length of each fixation
  - Total dwell time (ms): sum of all fixation durations on a region
  - First fixation location: x,y coordinates of first fixation after video onset
  - First fixation duration: how long the first fixation lasts

WHAT THEY TELL YOU:
  - Fixation count = visual search effort (more fixations = more searching)
  - Fixation duration = processing depth (longer = deeper processing)
  - Dwell time = overall attention allocation
  - First fixation = what captures attention initially

KEY COMPARISONS:
  a) Chosen vs unchosen video (do people look more at what they pick?)
  b) Correct vs incorrect trials (does gaze differ when right vs wrong?)
  c) Across 5 personality traits (does gaze effort differ by trait?)

STATISTICAL TESTS:
  - Paired t-test: chosen vs unchosen video fixation metrics
  - Repeated-measures ANOVA: fixation metrics across 5 traits
  - Post-hoc: Tukey HSD or Bonferroni-corrected pairwise comparisons
  - Effect sizes: Cohen's d for t-tests, partial eta-squared for ANOVA

HOW TO COMPUTE:
  1. Parse EDF -> extract fixation events (start_time, end_time, x, y, duration)
  2. Filter fixations occurring during video playback (use TRIAL_START/VIDEO_ONSET messages)
  3. Group by trial, compute count and mean duration
  4. Merge with behavioral data (chosen video, accuracy, trait)


========================================================================
METHOD 2: AREA OF INTEREST (AOI) ANALYSIS — MOST IMPORTANT FOR YOUR STUDY
========================================================================

PURPOSE:
  Determine which FACE REGIONS people look at when judging different
  personality traits. This is the core contribution of your eye tracking study.

AOI REGIONS TO DEFINE:
  - Left eye region (including eyebrow)
  - Right eye region (including eyebrow)
  - Nose bridge
  - Mouth region (lips + surrounding area)
  - Forehead
  - Chin / jaw line
  - Left cheek
  - Right cheek

HOW TO DEFINE AOIs ON VIDEO:
  Since your stimuli are face VIDEOS (not static images), the face moves
  across frames. You need per-frame AOI mapping:

  Step 1: Use MediaPipe Face Mesh (468 landmarks) or dlib (68 landmarks)
          to detect face landmarks on each video frame
  Step 2: Group landmarks into AOI regions:
          - Eyes: landmarks 33-46 (dlib) or mesh indices for eye contour
          - Nose: landmarks 27-35 (dlib)
          - Mouth: landmarks 48-68 (dlib)
          - etc.
  Step 3: Create bounding polygons around each AOI per frame
  Step 4: For each fixation, determine which frame was displayed at that time
  Step 5: Check if fixation (x,y) falls within any AOI polygon for that frame

  NOTE: If face movement is minimal (face-centered preprocessed videos),
        you can simplify by using average landmark positions across all frames.

KEY MEASURES PER AOI:
  - Dwell time proportion: % of total viewing time spent in each AOI
  - Entry count: how many times gaze enters each AOI (re-visits)
  - First AOI visited: which region captures first fixation
  - Mean fixation duration per AOI: processing depth per region

HYPOTHESIZED TRAIT-SPECIFIC PATTERNS:
  - Extraversion -> mouth region (smiling, expressiveness cues)
  - Emotional Stability -> eye region (anxiety, calmness signals)
  - Agreeableness -> overall face / mouth (warmth, friendliness cues)
  - Conscientiousness -> may not have strong regional bias
  - Openness -> may not have strong regional bias

STATISTICAL TESTS:
  - Two-way repeated-measures ANOVA: Trait (5) x AOI (6-8) on dwell proportion
  - Main effect of AOI: which region gets most attention overall?
  - Main effect of Trait: does total fixation effort differ by trait?
  - Interaction (Trait x AOI): THE KEY TEST — do people look at different
    regions for different traits?
  - Post-hoc simple effects: for each trait, which AOI dominates?

TOOLS:
  - MediaPipe: pip install mediapipe (Google's face mesh, 468 landmarks)
  - dlib: pip install dlib (68 landmarks, needs shape_predictor model file)
  - shapely: pip install shapely (for point-in-polygon AOI hit testing)


========================================================================
METHOD 3: GAZE CASCADE / GAZE BIAS EFFECT
========================================================================

PURPOSE:
  Test whether people look more at the face video they will eventually
  choose as having "more" of the personality trait. This replicates the
  classic gaze cascade effect (Shimojo et al., 2003) in personality context.

THE EFFECT:
  When choosing between two options, people progressively look more at the
  option they will select. This gaze bias grows over time and is strongest
  just before the decision.

APPLICATION TO YOUR SEQUENTIAL DESIGN:
  Your videos are shown sequentially (Video 1, then Video 2), not side-by-side.
  So the classic "gaze cascade" (simultaneous comparison) doesn't directly apply.

  Instead, measure:
  a) Total fixation intensity during the chosen video vs unchosen video
     (are people more "engaged" when watching the video they'll pick?)
  b) Last video thought about: which video was being viewed just before response?
  c) Fixation duration comparison: longer fixations during chosen video?
  d) Pupil response: more dilation during chosen video?

ANALYSES:
  1. Compare total dwell time during Video 1 vs Video 2, split by choice
  2. Compare mean fixation duration during chosen vs unchosen video
  3. Logistic regression: can gaze metrics during each video predict choice?
  4. If any "comparison screen" exists (decision phase): analyze gaze there

STATISTICAL TESTS:
  - Paired t-test: dwell time on chosen vs unchosen
  - Logistic mixed-effects model: gaze metrics predicting choice (1 or 2)
  - Within-subject correlation: gaze bias magnitude vs confidence

EXPECTED FINDING:
  People should show more intense/longer fixations during the video they
  ultimately choose as exhibiting the trait more strongly.


========================================================================
METHOD 4: PUPIL DILATION ANALYSIS
========================================================================

PURPOSE:
  Pupil size reflects cognitive effort, arousal, and decision difficulty.
  EyeLink 1000 Plus records pupil size at 1000 Hz — excellent temporal resolution.

PREPROCESSING PIPELINE:
  Step 1: Extract raw pupil data from EDF (timestamp, pupil_size)
  Step 2: Blink detection and removal
          - EyeLink marks blinks automatically
          - Remove samples during blinks (pupil = 0 or marked)
          - Extend removal window: 50ms before and 100ms after each blink
  Step 3: Interpolation
          - Linear interpolation across blink gaps
          - Only interpolate gaps < 500ms; discard trials with longer gaps
  Step 4: Baseline correction
          - Baseline period: last 500ms of pre-trial fixation cross
          - Subtract mean baseline from all subsequent samples
          - Result: relative pupil change from baseline
  Step 5: Low-pass filter
          - 4th order Butterworth filter, 4-10 Hz cutoff
          - Removes high-frequency noise
  Step 6: Downsample
          - From 1000 Hz to 50-100 Hz for analysis (reduces data volume)

KEY COMPARISONS:
  a) Correct vs incorrect trials:
     - Harder decisions (incorrect) should produce larger pupil dilation
     - Compare mean pupil during video viewing

  b) Across 5 traits:
     - Which trait causes most cognitive effort?
     - Hypothesis: traits harder to perceive -> larger pupil

  c) High confidence vs low confidence:
     - High confidence -> less cognitive effort -> smaller pupil?
     - Or high confidence -> strong engagement -> larger pupil?

  d) Timecourse analysis:
     - Plot average pupil trace aligned to video onset
     - When does peak dilation occur within each ~16s video?
     - Compare timecourses between conditions

STATISTICAL TESTS:
  - Paired t-test: mean pupil for correct vs incorrect
  - Repeated-measures ANOVA: mean pupil across 5 traits
  - Cluster-based permutation test: timecourse comparison
    (tests at which TIME POINTS conditions differ significantly,
     correcting for multiple comparisons across time)
  - Correlation: mean pupil vs response time (both reflect difficulty)

TOOLS:
  - scipy.signal: butterworth filter, filtering
  - numpy: interpolation, baseline correction
  - mne-python (optional): cluster-based permutation tests on timecourses

METHOD 5: SCANPATH ANALYSIS
========================================================================

PURPOSE:
  Analyze the SEQUENCE of fixations, not just their locations.
  Do people scan faces in a consistent order when judging a specific trait?

METHODS:

  a) ScanMatch (Cristino et al., 2010):
     - Converts scanpath to a string (each AOI = a letter)
     - Example: "E E M N E M" (eyes, eyes, mouth, nose, eyes, mouth)
     - Computes string-edit distance (Needleman-Wunsch) between scanpaths
     - Lower distance = more similar scanpaths

  b) MultiMatch (Dewhurst et al., 2012):
     - Compares scanpaths on 5 dimensions simultaneously:
       1. Shape (overall path shape)
       2. Direction (angle between consecutive fixations)
       3. Length (saccade amplitudes)
       4. Position (absolute fixation locations)
       5. Duration (fixation durations)
     - Returns similarity score for each dimension

  c) Recurrence Quantification Analysis (RQA):
     - Measures how often gaze returns to previously visited locations
     - Recurrence rate: proportion of fixations that revisit old locations
     - Determinism: how predictable is the gaze sequence?
     - Laminarity: does gaze get "stuck" in certain regions?

USE FOR YOUR STUDY:
  1. Within-participant consistency: does the same person scan similarly
     across different videos of the same trait?
  2. Between-participant consistency: do different people scan similarly
     when judging the same trait?
  3. Trait-specific scanpaths: are scanpath patterns different for
     Extraversion vs Conscientiousness?

STATISTICAL TESTS:
  - Permutation test on scanpath similarity (compare within-trait vs
    between-trait similarity)
  - ANOVA on scanpath similarity scores across traits
  - Correlation: scanpath consistency vs accuracy


========================================================================
METHOD 6: GAZE ENTROPY
========================================================================

PURPOSE:
  Quantify how spread out or focused the gaze distribution is.
  Entropy = measure of uncertainty/randomness in gaze allocation.

TYPES:

  a) Stationary (spatial) entropy:
     - Divide screen into grid cells or use AOI regions
     - Calculate proportion of fixations in each cell/AOI
     - Shannon entropy: H = -sum(p_i * log2(p_i))
     - Range: 0 (all fixations in one spot) to log2(N) (perfectly uniform)

  b) Gaze transition entropy:
     - Based on transition probabilities between AOIs
     - How predictable is the next fixation location?
     - Low transition entropy = highly predictable scan pattern
     - High transition entropy = random/exploratory scanning

INTERPRETATION:
  - Low entropy = focused, efficient viewing (viewer knows where to look)
  - High entropy = exploratory, uncertain viewing (searching for information)
  - Correct trials may have lower entropy (efficient strategy)
  - Easy traits may have lower entropy (diagnostic info is obvious)

HYPOTHESES:
  - Extraversion (most perceptible) -> lowest entropy (focus on mouth/smile)
  - Openness/Conscientiousness (less perceptible) -> higher entropy
  - Correct trials -> lower entropy than incorrect trials
  - High confidence -> lower entropy than low confidence

STATISTICAL TESTS:
  - One-way repeated-measures ANOVA: entropy across 5 traits
  - Paired t-test: entropy for correct vs incorrect
  - Correlation: entropy vs accuracy, entropy vs confidence
  - Mixed-effects model: entropy ~ trait * accuracy + (1|participant)


========================================================================
METHOD 7: TRANSITION MATRIX ANALYSIS
========================================================================

PURPOSE:
  Build probability matrices of gaze transitions between face AOIs.
  Reveals the "flow" of visual attention across the face.

HOW TO BUILD:
  1. Label each fixation with its AOI (eyes, nose, mouth, etc.)
  2. Create sequences: E -> M -> N -> E -> E -> M ...
  3. Count transitions: how often does gaze go from region A to region B?
  4. Normalize rows to get transition probabilities

EXAMPLE MATRIX (Extraversion trials):
           Eyes   Nose   Mouth  Forehead  Other
  Eyes      --    0.15   0.45    0.10     0.30
  Nose     0.35    --    0.40    0.05     0.20
  Mouth    0.50   0.15    --     0.05     0.30
  Forehead 0.40   0.20   0.30    --       0.10

  Reading: from Eyes, 45% of transitions go to Mouth (strong link)

ANALYSES:
  1. Build one transition matrix per trait (averaged across participants)
  2. Compare matrices: do transition patterns differ by trait?
  3. Identify dominant transitions for each trait
  4. Network visualization: nodes = AOIs, edges = transition probabilities
  5. Compare: correct vs incorrect trial transition matrices

STATISTICAL TESTS:
  - Chi-square test: are transition frequencies different from uniform?
  - Markov chain analysis: is the gaze sequence Markovian (memoryless)?
  - Permutation tests: compare transition matrices between traits
  - Graph theory metrics: identify hub AOIs (most connected regions)


========================================================================
METHOD 8: MIXED-EFFECTS MODELS (YOUR MAIN STATISTICAL FRAMEWORK)
========================================================================

PURPOSE:
  With 51 participants x 125 trials, you have a nested data structure.
  Mixed-effects models properly account for:
  - Repeated measures within participants
  - Individual differences between participants
  - Item (video pair) variability

MODEL STRUCTURE:
  Fixed effects: what you manipulate/measure
  Random effects: sources of variability you want to control for

EXAMPLE MODELS FOR YOUR STUDY:

  Model 1 - Fixation duration by trait:
    fixation_duration ~ trait + (1 | participant) + (1 | video_pair)

  Model 2 - Gaze predicting accuracy:
    accuracy ~ dwell_time_chosen + fixation_count + trait + (1 | participant)

  Model 3 - AOI analysis:
    dwell_proportion ~ trait * aoi_region + (1 + trait | participant)

  Model 4 - Pupil and confidence:
    pupil_dilation ~ trait * confidence + (1 | participant) + (1 | video_pair)

  Model 5 - Full model:
    accuracy ~ trait + fixation_duration + gaze_entropy + pupil_dilation +
               confidence + (1 + trait | participant) + (1 | video_pair)

RANDOM EFFECTS EXPLAINED:
  - (1 | participant): each person has their own baseline (random intercept)
  - (1 + trait | participant): each person has their own baseline AND their
    own sensitivity to different traits (random intercept + slope)
  - (1 | video_pair): each video pair has its own difficulty level

TOOLS:
  Python: statsmodels.formula.api.mixedlm()
  R (more powerful): lme4::lmer() or lmerTest::lmer()
  Python alternative: pymer4 (Python wrapper for R's lme4)

WHY NOT JUST USE ANOVA:
  - ANOVA requires aggregating data (losing trial-level information)
  - Mixed models use ALL individual trials
  - Mixed models handle missing data and unbalanced designs
  - Mixed models estimate participant AND item variability simultaneously
