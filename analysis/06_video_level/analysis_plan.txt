06 - VIDEO-LEVEL ANALYSIS
==========================

PURPOSE: Analyze performance at the individual video/pair level to understand
which specific stimuli are easy or hard to judge and why.

DATA NEEDED:
- Behavioral data with video identifiers (video_first, video_second columns)
- Accuracy and RT per trial

ANALYSES TO RUN:
----------------

1. VIDEO PAIR DIFFICULTY
   - For each of the 125 unique video pairs (25 per trait):
     Calculate % of participants who answered correctly
   - Identify easiest pairs (>80% accuracy) and hardest pairs (<50% accuracy)
   - Are some high-low pairs more discriminable than others?

2. INDIVIDUAL VIDEO "STRENGTH"
   - For each of the 50 videos: how often is it correctly identified?
   - High videos: how often chosen as "more [trait]"? (should be high)
   - Low videos: how often NOT chosen? (should be high)
   - Calculate "video discriminability" score
   - Are some high-trait videos weak exemplars? Some low-trait videos ambiguous?

3. VIDEO PRESENTATION ORDER EFFECTS
   - Compare accuracy when high video is shown first vs second
   - Primacy effect: are first-shown videos chosen more often?
   - Recency effect: are second-shown videos chosen more often?
   - This tests for bias in the sequential presentation design

4. VIDEO-LEVEL GAZE ANALYSIS
   - Which videos attract longer fixations?
   - Pupil dilation differences across videos
   - Do "easy" videos (high accuracy) produce different gaze patterns?

5. STIMULUS VALIDATION
   - Are the high/low labels from the dataset valid?
   - If accuracy is near chance for some pairs, the stimuli may not differ enough
   - Report item-level statistics for stimulus quality assessment

STATISTICAL METHODS:
- Descriptive statistics per video/pair
- Binomial test per pair (accuracy vs 50%)
- Paired t-test (first vs second position accuracy)
- Item-response theory (IRT) for video difficulty estimation (advanced)

OUTPUT FILES TO GENERATE:
- video_pair_accuracy.csv
- video_discriminability.csv
- presentation_order_effect.png
- hardest_easiest_pairs.png
